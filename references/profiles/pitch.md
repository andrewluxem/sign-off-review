# Profile: pitch

**Use for:** consulting proposals, statements of work, outreach to prospects.
**Signals:** a fee or price, scope or phases, a "why us" section, a named prospect.

## Seat 1: Decision (economic buyer)

- **1.1** The first screen says what the buyer gets, what it costs, why this provider and why now.
- **1.2** The outcome is stated in the buyer's terms, as results the buyer will see, not as activities or deliverables the provider produces.
- **1.3** The next step is one clear action with a date.
- **1.4** The pitch is written for this buyer: it uses facts about their business that a generic proposal would not contain.

## Seat 2: Logic

- **2.1** Scope, steps and the promised outcome line up: every promised outcome has the work that produces it, and every piece of work serves an outcome.
- **2.2** The timeline lets the outcome be produced and observed within the engagement, or the pitch says when it will be observed.
- **2.3** What the buyer must provide (data access, approvals, team time) is stated, with the step that needs it.
- **2.4** The engagement has a clear end: what is handed over, and what the buyer can do with it afterward.

## Seat 3: Evidence

- **3.1** Every result claimed is the author's own work, or the author's share of it is stated.
- **3.2** Every result is attributable: the client is named with permission, or described specifically enough to check, and the pitch says the result may be cited.
- **3.3** Every result has a baseline and a timeframe: from what, to what, over what period.
- **3.4** Nothing is under NDA or confidential to a past client, including figures that would identify the client.
- **3.5** Every benchmark or market figure has a source fetched this session, or goes under Missing outside view.

## Seat 4: Skeptic (procurement)

- **4.1** Exclusions are stated: build work, tooling, media, creative, travel, and anything else the buyer might assume is included.
- **4.2** Who does the work is named: people or roles, seniority and time committed.
- **4.3** The price has visible logic: the days, phases or deliverables it is built from, and what would change it.
- **4.4** Risk is addressed: what happens if the outcome is not reached, what the buyer can cancel, what is and is not guaranteed.
- **4.5** Payment terms, term length and ownership of the work product are stated.

## Seat 5: Outsider (non-specialist)

- **5.1** A reader outside CRM and lifecycle marketing, such as a finance lead or the CEO, understands what will change for the business.
- **5.2** Specialist terms (journeys, segments, cohorts, deliverability) are explained on first use.
- **5.3** The pitch stands alone. It does not depend on a sales call the reader did not attend.

## Tie-out scope

The fee, dates, durations, team size and every result figure, across the pitch and any attachments.

## Outside view

Check every benchmark or lift figure against a source fetched this session. If none can be fetched, list it under Missing outside view as unverified. A promised outcome with no comparison (what similar engagements achieved) goes there too. Never supply a figure.

## Premortem prompts

Internal (on the author's side):
- A result claim the author cannot defend in the room.
- Scope promised beyond what the team can deliver.
- The pitch sells the author's method instead of solving the buyer's problem.
- The contact who receives it has no budget authority.

External:
- The buyer's data or platform access arrives late.
- An internal team proposes to do the work instead.
- A budget freeze.

## False alarms (pitch)

- A short pitch for a small engagement.
- No case-study section, when the result claims carry their own attribution.
- A fixed fee without a day rate, when the deliverables and exclusions are stated.
- A confident tone. Flag unsupported claims, not confidence.
