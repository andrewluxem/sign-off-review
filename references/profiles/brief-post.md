# Profile: brief-post

**Use for:** newsletter briefs, LinkedIn posts, and posts repurposed from a longer piece.
**Signals:** a thesis, a hook, a takeaway for the reader, external facts or statistics.

## Seat 1: Decision (busy reader)

- **1.1** The thesis lands before the fold: within the first two or three lines of a post, or the first paragraph of a brief.
- **1.2** The piece tells the reader what to do or think differently, specifically enough to act on.
- **1.3** The headline or hook promises what the body delivers. The body does not pivot to a different point.
- **1.4** The intended reader can be identified from the piece itself.

## Seat 2: Logic

- **2.1** The argument chains: each step follows from the one before, with no missing link between evidence and conclusion.
- **2.2** No leap from anecdote to rule. A single case, client or experience is presented as one case, and a general rule rests on more than that.
- **2.3** The reach of the conclusion matches the reach of the evidence: one channel, one industry, one period.
- **2.4** The takeaway follows from the argument actually made, not from a different, unstated one.

## Seat 3: Evidence

- **3.1** Every external fact (statistic, study, quote, event) carries the URL that was actually fetched for it.
- **3.2** No vague attributions. "Studies show", "experts say", "research finds" and "data suggests" name the study, the expert or the dataset.
- **3.3** Every statistic carries its population, date and measure: who was surveyed or measured, when, and on what.
- **3.4** First-person results say what the author did, for whom, when, and compared with what.

## Seat 4: Skeptic (dissenting practitioner)

- **4.1** Write the strongest counterargument a practitioner who disagrees would make. Does the piece meet it, or narrow its claim so the counterargument no longer applies?
- **4.2** The measure fits the claim: a revenue claim is measured in revenue, not in opens or clicks.
- **4.3** Other explanations for the result (novelty, seasonality, selection, a change made at the same time) are ruled out or acknowledged.
- **4.4** The advice holds for the readers it addresses, or the piece says where it does not apply.

## Seat 5: Outsider (non-specialist)

- **5.1** A reader outside the author's field can follow the argument. Jargon is explained on first use.
- **5.2** The voice is the author's: claims are specific, first-hand and owned, not generic statements anyone could have posted.
- **5.3** Line-level prose (AI tells, filler phrases, monotone rhythm) is not reviewed here. If present, record one Minor finding with the fix "Run avoid-ai-writing on the revised draft", and quote no rewrites.

## Tie-out scope

Every figure, date and name in the piece, including the title, subtitle and any image text or caption passed in.

## Outside view

Check every benchmark or statistic against a source fetched this session. A first-person result with no comparison (a control, a prior period, other segments) goes under Missing outside view. Never supply a figure.

## Premortem prompts

Internal (on the author's side):
- The author generalized from their own experience.
- The claim is broader than the author can defend in the comments.
- The piece repeats a take the audience has already seen, so it adds nothing.
- The author holds the view for reasons that are not on the page.

External:
- A practitioner posts a counterexample in the comments.
- A cited statistic is debunked, or its link is dead.
- Platform truncation hides the thesis.

## False alarms (brief-post)

- No counterargument section, when the claim is narrow and scoped.
- A personal story used as illustration, when it is not the only support for a general rule.
- A short post. Length should suit the platform.
- Informal tone, contractions or one-line paragraphs.
- No URL for the author's own first-hand experience. It needs specifics (who, when, compared with what), not a link.
