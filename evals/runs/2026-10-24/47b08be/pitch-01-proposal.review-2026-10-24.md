# Sign-off review: pitch-01-proposal.md, profile pitch, stage 3
**Verdict:** Return
**In one line:** The headline promise of a 15 percent repeat-purchase lift has no baseline or basis, and the 12-week scope, which ends in a deck and a workshop, cannot produce it, so Pellucid cannot decide to hire on this draft yet.
**Question:** Should Pellucid Pet Foods hire Ostler & Finch for this engagement? (given)
**Answer the draft gives:** Yes: hire Ostler & Finch for a 12-week, $48,000 engagement that "lifts Pellucid's repeat purchase rate by 15 percent."
**Assumptions:** Profile `pitch` was detected from the fee, the phased scope, the "Why Ostler & Finch" section and the named prospect, and was not confirmed with the user. Stage 3 (final) is assumed by default. The review date of 2026-10-24 is used for every deadline check, and the draft is treated as not yet sent. The question is asked from the buyer's side, so the verdict says whether this draft can support that decision. No source was fetched this session because the draft cites no third-party benchmark and names no client to check. All five seats ran as parallel subagents, and none ran inline.

## Missing outside view
- **15 percent repeat-purchase lift:** nothing to compare it with. There is no Pellucid baseline, no result from comparable engagements and no benchmark. The draft cites no source, and none was fetched this session. The hire decision rests on this figure (finding 2).
- **212 percent email revenue growth:** unverified. The client is unnamed, so there is no source to fetch, and it measures email revenue, so it cannot serve as a comparison for the 15 percent repeat-purchase promise (findings 2 and 3).
- **$48,000 fee:** nothing to compare it with. The draft does not say what the lift is worth to Pellucid or what similar engagements cost (finding 2).
- **"More than a decade" across pet, home and beauty:** unverified, with no named clients. No decision driver rests on it alone, so it is not a finding.

## Premortem
1. In the first meeting, Mara or her finance lead asks how a deck and a workshop produce a 15 percent lift, from what baseline, and who the 212 percent client was. Ostler & Finch cannot answer in the room, the pitch loses credibility and the deal stalls. (internal)
2. Access to Pellucid's email, SMS and customer data arrives weeks after kickoff, so the three-week audit runs into the strategy phase and the handover deck rests on partial data.
3. Pellucid's own ecommerce team argues it can design the onboarding, replenishment and winback journeys itself, since it must build them after the deck anyway, and the budget goes to implementation instead.

## What is good and must survive
- The three phases with week ranges (Audit weeks 1 to 3, Strategy weeks 4 to 8, Handover weeks 9 to 12) add up to exactly 12 weeks and give the fee build-up, buyer inputs and an exit point something to attach to.
- One fixed fee with a plain split, half at signing and half at handover, that a finance lead can read at a glance.
- Opening on a metric the buyer cares about, the repeat purchase rate, instead of a list of activities.
- The named journey types (onboarding, replenishment, winback) with segment rules and timing, which tie the strategy work directly to repeat purchase.
- The audit's defined data window, "the last 12 months of campaign results", which can supply the baseline the 15 percent figure is missing.
- A proof point from the pet category, the right sector for this buyer once it is attributed.

## What fixed looks like
The opening says what the 12 weeks deliver. Any lift figure carries:
- Pellucid's current repeat purchase rate and its source
- a stated unit
- a measurement window after launch
- the party that builds and launches the journeys
- the basis for the number

The 212 percent result is either cited with the client's permission, a baseline, a period and Ostler & Finch's role, or it is cut. The next step is dated after the send date, names the action and the contact, and states the price validity and the end date. The draft also names:
- at least two facts about Pellucid's business and why it should start now
- what Pellucid must provide, and by which week
- the handover artifacts and who implements after week 12
- what the fee excludes
- the team and their time
- what is guaranteed, and how Pellucid can exit after the audit
- who owns the work product
