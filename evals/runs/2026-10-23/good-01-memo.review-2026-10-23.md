# Sign-off review: good-01-memo.md, profile strategy, stage 3
**Verdict:** Release with fixes
**In one line:** The memo answers the question and every figure ties, but before it goes to the board the stop rule needs a test that visitors who merely arrive later cannot pass, the Saturday pay basis behind "fits inside the budget" needs a source, and the other Saturday options need ruling out.
**Question:** Should the board approve a six-month trial of Saturday afternoon hours at Eastgate Library? (given)
**Answer the draft gives:** "Approve the trial. It costs $8,411, fits inside the unspent hours-extension budget, and stops at the end of March if afternoon visits stay low." This matches the question.
**Assumptions:** Profile `strategy` was detected from the content (a decision requested, options, costs) and not confirmed, because the user could not be asked. Stage 3 (final) is assumed, the default. The five seats ran as independent subagents, each given only its own section of the profile.

## Missing outside view
- Westbrook comparison (stop-rule bar). It is sourced to an internal district report (Westbrook gate counter report, December 2024), but the draft does not say whether 63 percent is a three-month average or the month-three level, or which months it covers, so it is not shown to be like-for-like with Eastgate's 13-Saturday average. Not verified this session; the strategy profile does not require a fetch. See finding 5.
- Seasonal base for the bar. The 26.5 bar rests on the September 2025 to August 2026 average, with no January to March Saturday figure from Eastgate's own gate counter to compare it with. See finding 4.
- Survey conversion. The 46 percent stated intent has no base rate for how often stated intent becomes visits. The draft says so openly and uses a stop rule instead of a forecast, so this is not a finding.
- Trial cost. The $8,411 is not compared with what Westbrook's added hours cost. Not a finding: building costs are $1,040 of the total, so even a large error there leaves the trial inside the $12,000 line, and the larger cost risk (pay basis) is finding 2.

## Premortem
1. Patrons do not learn of the new hours before January, because no publicity is planned or costed, so the Saturdays that count toward the March test start quiet and the trial stops on a low average. (internal)
2. Afternoon demand from January to March runs below the full-year pattern the 26.5 bar is built on, so the trial stops on a seasonal low rather than on weak demand.
3. The rota cannot cover three extra staff each Saturday within the two-long-Saturdays-a-month limit in the five-Saturday months of January and May, so cover comes from relief or premium-paid shifts and staff costs overrun the $8,411. (internal)

## What is good and must survive
- The Recommendation opens with "Approve the trial." and the next sentence gives the cost, the budget fit and the stop date, so a reader who stops there knows what is asked.
- The cost table shows the basis and a dated source for each line, and its arithmetic holds: 3 x 3 x 26 = 234 hours, 234 x $31.50 = $7,371, $40 x 26 = $1,040, total $8,411.
- "Survey answers overstate real visits, so the trial carries a stop rule instead of a forecast." The draft names the weakness in its own evidence and answers it with a testable bar.
- The stop rule reduced to a concrete bar (26.5 visitors per hour), a fixed check point (13 Saturdays, through 27 March) and a capped spend (about $4,200, half of $8,411). Fixes to findings 1 and 4 must keep it this concrete.
- Exact dates: 2 January and 26 June 2027 are both Saturdays, 26 in all, and the 13 through March match the early-stop spend.
- Sunday opening and weekday evenings are each ruled out with a reason a board member can check (1.5 times the rate on Sundays; 18 visits per hour after 18:00).
