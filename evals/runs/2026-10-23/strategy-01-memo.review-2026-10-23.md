# Sign-off review: strategy-01-memo.md, profile strategy, stage 3
**Verdict:** Return
**In one line:** The Summary sells a $1.4M annual saving and a payback under six months that the memo's own $1.1M table does not support, so the committee cannot approve on these numbers until they are reconciled.
**Question:** Should Brindlewood close the Boise distribution center and serve its stores from Reno by 30 June 2027? (given)
**Answer the draft gives:** Yes: "We recommend closing Boise and serving its 38 stores from Reno," saving $1.4M a year from FY2028 against a one-time cost of $600K.
**Assumptions:** Profile `strategy` was detected from the content (a decision requested, options, costs, savings) and could not be confirmed with the user. Stage 3 (final) is the default because no stage was given. The question is taken as given from the request. The answer matches the question.

## Missing outside view
- Annual saving ($1.1M in the table, $1.4M in the Summary): not compared with any past Brindlewood closure or consolidation result. A decision driver rests on it (findings 1 and 7).
- "Industry benchmarks ... 20 to 30 percent": no publisher, date or sample is named, and it was not verified this session. Per the strategy profile it is flagged, not replaced. It also cannot be applied, because the draft gives no logistics cost base (finding 7).
- Reno capacity: the 61 percent is an August 2026 reading, with no comparison to Reno's past peak-season utilization or a past absorption of added volume (finding 2).
- Hiring 14 Reno roles by May 2027: no comparison with Reno's past time to fill roles (finding 4).
- In-stock effect of moving from next-day to two-day delivery: no comparison with any store region already served at two-day, or a past service change (finding 3).
- One-time cost of $600K: no comparison with the cost of a past inventory move or site exit (findings 5 and 6).

## Premortem
1. The committee spots that the Summary's $1.4M does not match the table's $1.1M and sends the memo back, and the next meeting falls after the renewal response deadline (which the memo never states), so the lease outcome is decided by default instead of by choice. (internal)
2. No one owns the transition ("The team will manage the transition"), so the 14 Reno hires and the March to June 2027 inventory moves slip, and the Boise lease ends on 31 July 2027 before Reno is ready to serve the 38 stores. (internal)
3. Reno, measured at 61 percent in August, runs short of capacity at the holiday peak once it carries Boise's 29 percent of network units, in-stock rates fall in Boise-region stores, and lost sales eat into a saving that was never stress-tested.

## What is good and must survive
- The "Decision requested" line and the Summary's first sentence state the action, the 38 stores and the 30 June 2027 date before anything else.
- "Why now" ties the timing to dated, sourced documents (the lease file, the renewal notice dated 2 June 2026, the August 2026 warehouse system utilization report). Use that citation style as the model for the other figures.
- The savings table itemizes each line, shows the added line-haul as a negative and labor net of the 14 Reno roles, and its rows sum correctly to $1.1M (635 + 430 + 120 - 85).
- The one-time cost is split into three named items that sum to $600K (310 + 190 + 100), with a spending window of March to June 2027.
- The lease figures tie: a 22 percent rise to $635K implies about $520K current rent and the $115K increase stated under Options considered.

## What fixed looks like
The Summary, the savings table and the payback all use one reconciled annual saving, and any line that explains a difference from the table's $1.1M sits in the table with its source. The payback is recomputed from that figure, with its start month stated. That alone moves the verdict to Release with fixes. To reach Release, the memo also shows Reno's utilization after the move at the holiday peak, a downside case with lost sales netted against the saving and a named reversal trigger, the renewal response deadline and the fallback if Reno is not staffed by 31 July 2027, a named accountable owner with the $600K budget in the ask, and a source or assumption label on every saving and one-time line.
