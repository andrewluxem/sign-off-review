# Sign-off review: good-01-memo.md, profile strategy, stage 3
**Verdict:** Release with fixes
**In one line:** The memo answers the question, every figure ties and the costs are sourced, but the stop rule cannot tell new visitors from current ones arriving later, and the memo never rules out moving the existing hours at no cost; fix those two before it goes to the board.
**Question:** Should the board approve a six-month trial of Saturday afternoon hours at Eastgate Library? (given)
**Answer the draft gives:** "Approve the trial. It costs $8,411, fits inside the unspent hours-extension budget, and stops at the end of March if afternoon visits stay low."
**Assumptions:** Profile `strategy` was detected from the content (decision requested, options, costs) and not confirmed with the author. Stage 3 (final) is the default and was not confirmed. ## Missing outside view
- Saturday afternoon uptake: the only comparison is Westbrook (one peer, cited to the Westbrook gate counter report, December 2024). The draft does not show that Westbrook is comparable or that its 63 percent measures the same thing as Eastgate's 13-Saturday average. See finding 4.
- Added visits: no reference shows whether Westbrook's afternoon hours raised its total Saturday visits or only moved existing ones. See finding 1.
- Season: no January to March Saturday baseline for Eastgate to set against the trial window. See finding 5.
- Cost per afternoon: no past result, such as Westbrook's actual staffing and building cost, checks the 3-staff and $40 inputs. See finding 9.
- Survey intent: the 46 percent has no base rate for how often stated intent turns into visits. The draft says so openly ("Survey answers overstate real visits") and builds no forecast on it, so this is not a finding.

## Premortem
1. Afternoon counts clear the 26.5 bar in March because the visitors who now crowd the last hour simply arrive later, so the board extends the hours in April and later carries a full-year cost with no rise in total Saturday visits. The stop rule measured what the author hoped to see. (internal)
2. Eastgate cannot fill the rota within the two-long-Saturdays-a-month cap once winter leave and sickness hit, so afternoons run short-staffed or close early and the March numbers reflect the staffing gap, not demand. (internal)
3. Winter weather and seasonally low January to March traffic hold afternoon visits under the bar, so the trial stops at the end of March although spring demand would have cleared it.

## What is good and must survive
- The "Decision requested" line names the exact hours, the date range and the number of Saturdays in one sentence.
- The Recommendation paragraph gives the answer, the cost, the funding source, the stop point, the owner ("I will run it") and the report date in four sentences.
- The cost table shows a basis and a dated source on every line, and the total reconciles: $7,371 + $1,040 = $8,411, the same figure in the recommendation, the table and the budget sentence.
- The stop rule is a single number (26.5 visitors per hour) derived from stated inputs, with a fixed decision point after 13 Saturdays and a loss capped at about $4,200. The fixes for findings 1 and 4 should add to this rule, not replace it.
- "Survey answers overstate real visits, so the trial carries a stop rule instead of a forecast." This names the weakness in the survey evidence and answers it with a design choice.
- Demand rests on two kinds of evidence: a dated gate counter export (what visitors did) and a dated, sized survey (what they said).
