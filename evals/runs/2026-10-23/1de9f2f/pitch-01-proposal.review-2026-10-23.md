# Sign-off review: pitch-01-proposal.md, profile pitch, stage 3
**Verdict:** Return
**In one line:** Not on this draft: it promises a 15 percent repeat-purchase lift that its own scope (an audit, journey designs, a deck and a workshop) cannot produce or measure, and it leaves out the basic terms procurement needs, so Pellucid cannot make the hiring decision from it.
**Question:** Should Pellucid Pet Foods hire Ostler & Finch for this engagement? (given)
**Answer the draft gives:** Yes, hire us for a 12-week, $48,000 engagement that "lifts Pellucid's repeat purchase rate by 15 percent", backed by a 212 percent email revenue result for "a national pet retailer" (an answer that matches the question).
**Assumptions:** Profile `pitch` was detected from the fee, the phased scope, the "Why Ostler & Finch" section and the named prospect, and was not confirmed. Stage 3 (final) by default. The question is read as the decision the pitch supports, so the verdict says whether this draft can go to Pellucid, not whether Pellucid should sign once it is fixed. The draft is assumed unsent and is reviewed on 23 October 2026 (see finding 7). No benchmark source was fetched this session, because the draft names no source, client or URL to fetch.

- The 212 percent email revenue growth is unverified: no source was fetched this session because the draft names no client, source or URL to check. Raised as findings 4 and 5.
- The $48,000 fee is compared with neither the value of the promised lift nor comparable engagements. Raised as findings 2 and 12.
- "More than a decade, across pet, home and beauty" is an unverified credential with no source to fetch. Not a finding: the case for this provider rests on the 212 percent result, not on tenure.

## Premortem
1. In the meeting, Mara or her finance lead asks how a deck and a workshop produce a 15 percent lift and what the 212 percent was measured from, Ostler & Finch cannot defend either number, and the whole proposal loses credibility. (internal)
2. Pellucid's own ecommerce or CRM team proposes to do the audit and journey design itself, because Pellucid would have to build and run the journeys anyway and the pitch shows no Pellucid-specific insight the team lacks.
3. Platform and data access arrive late, the three-week audit runs into the strategy weeks, and the fixed 12-week end and the handover invoice turn into a dispute.

## What is good and must survive
- The three named phases with week ranges that sum to the stated 12 weeks (Audit 1 to 3, Strategy 4 to 8, Handover 9 to 12), each with a plain verb. Every seat kept this.
- One fixed fee, $48,000, with a two-part payment schedule tied to signing and handover, and no open-ended hourly billing.
- Repeat purchase rate as the headline metric: a result the buyer sees, not provider activity.
- The named journeys (onboarding, replenishment and winback), which target repeat purchase directly and bound the strategy work.
- The audit's use of the last 12 months of campaign results, which can supply the baseline the 15 percent target needs.
- The one-page length, which a CEO can read in two minutes.

## What fixed looks like
"What you get" states what the 12 weeks deliver (audit findings, journey designs with segment rules and timing in a form Pellucid can build from, the deck and the workshop), who builds and launches the journeys afterward and at what cost, and the repeat-purchase lift expected after launch: defined against a stated baseline or one the audit sets, relative or in points, over a named window, sourced to past results, and labelled as a target or as a guarantee with its remedy. The 212 percent case is either cleared for citation with its baseline, period, metric and Ostler & Finch's share stated, or cut. The pitch opens with Pellucid's situation and why November matters, carries a live reply-by date and a named contact, and includes the procurement basics: exclusions, the named team and its time, the fee broken down by phase, buyer inputs with due dates, a dated handover with acceptance criteria, cancellation terms and ownership of the work.
