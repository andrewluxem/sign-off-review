## Missing outside view
- "73 percent of marketers say send time is the biggest lever on open rate" (P2): the draft names no source. A web search this session for the exact figure returned no source for it, so it is unverified. It also compares nothing: it records belief, not open rates by send hour. A decision driver rests on it; see finding 2.
- "Studies show that subscribers decide what to open in the first minutes of their day" (P2): no study is named, so nothing could be fetched, and the claim is unverified. A decision driver rests on it; see findings 2 and 6.
- "opens rose 18 percent in the first month" (P3): a first-person result with no control, no stated baseline period and no other segments. A decision driver rests on it; see finding 3.
- 6 a.m. against other hours: the only comparison in the draft is the retailer's former 10 a.m. send. No base rate or test covers any other hour, so "or don't bother" is compared with nothing. A decision driver rests on it; see findings 1 and 6.

## Premortem
1. The author generalized one furniture retailer's single before-and-after move into a rule for every campaign and cannot defend that scope when readers ask what else was tested. (internal)
2. A practitioner replies with their own send-time test in which 6 a.m. lost, or in which opens rose but orders did not, and the brief's one piece of advice is publicly undercut.
3. A reader asks for the source of the 73 percent figure or the "studies", none can be produced, and Lark & Ledger loses credibility with the retention marketers it is written for.

## What is good and must survive
- The bolded "What to do on Monday" line: one concrete action a skimmer can find, which is the form the question asks for. Narrow it; do not remove it.
- The masthead line "a weekly brief for retention marketers", which names the reader in one line.
- The first-hand case's specifics: "Last year I advised", the type of business and send (regional furniture retailer, weekly promotion), both send times (10 a.m. to 6 a.m.) and a stated window ("the first month"). Add the comparison and the downstream result to it; do not replace it.
- The length and single focus: one argument, readable in under a minute.

## What fixed looks like
The title, opening and Monday action claim no more than the evidence: a test of 6 a.m. against the current hour on scheduled promotional sends, with a holdout, a success metric beyond opens and a date to read the result, and with triggered flows and subscribers with no known time zone named as out of scope. Every external figure carries a fetched source with its population, date and measure, or is cut. The furniture case states its months, comparison, metric definition, time-zone setup and downstream result, and the piece says why 6 a.m. rather than another early hour, or drops the "first email in the inbox wins" mechanism.
