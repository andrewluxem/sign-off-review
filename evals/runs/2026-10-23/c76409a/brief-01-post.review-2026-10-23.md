## Numbers that do not tie
None.

## Missing outside view
- "73 percent of marketers say send time is the biggest lever on open rate": the draft names no source, and a web search this session found no origin for the figure. Unverified. See findings 2 and 6.
- "Studies show that subscribers decide what to open in the first minutes of their day": no study is named, so there is nothing to fetch or check. Unverified. See finding 7.
- The 18 percent open lift is compared with nothing: no control, no prior period, no other segment. See finding 4.
- "The first email in the inbox wins": no base rate or test links inbox position to opens. See finding 5.
- The open lift is never compared with a retention outcome such as orders or repeat purchase. See finding 3.

## Premortem
1. The author generalized from one client, one weekly promotion and one month of opens to "every campaign," and cannot defend that reach when readers ask for a control, a second case or a revenue number. (internal)
2. A practitioner posts a counterexample in the comments, such as a send-time test on their own list where a later hour won, and the brief has only one anecdote to answer with.
3. A reader asks where the 73 percent figure and the "studies" come from, the author cannot produce a source, and the brief loses credibility with the readers it most needs.

## What is good and must survive
- The bold "What to do on Monday" line: one action and one hour, easy for a skimmer to find.
- The first-hand case with a concrete before and after (weekly promotion moved from 10 a.m. to 6 a.m., measured over the first month), owned by the author ("Last year I advised") and presented as one practitioner's experience, not a study.
- The subtitle that names the reader: "a weekly brief for retention marketers."
- The subscriber-local-time detail in the action.
- Short, plain sentences a non-specialist can follow in under a minute.

## What fixed looks like
The Monday action matches the evidence. It covers one recurring promotional send, moved to 6 a.m. in the subscriber's local time and tested against the current send time with a holdout, a named metric and a duration, and it names the send types and audiences it does not cover. The retailer case states its month, its baseline, its measure (rate or count, relative or points) and a downstream result such as clicks or orders. The 73 percent figure and the "studies" either carry fetched sources with population and date or are cut. The title no longer says every other hour is worthless.
